    Before you land — bring or buy
      Airalo eSIM, step by step

      Airalo is an app that sells prepaid data plans for other countries as an eSIM, so you don't swap SIM cards or pay roaming. Works on any eSIM-capable, unlocked phone (iPhone XS or newer, Pixel 3+, Galaxy S20+).

      • Download the Airalo app, make an account, search Egypt.
      • Buy the plan. Brad: Egypt 15-day unlimited, $49. Duane: Egypt 10-day, $35.
      • Tap Install and follow the steps while still on home wifi. It adds a second line. Label it "Egypt". Leave it off for now.
      • When the plane lands: Settings → Cellular → turn the Egypt line on, set it as the data line, turn on Data Roaming for that line only. Turn data roaming off on the home line but keep the line on so texts and codes still arrive.
      • Data only, no Egyptian number. WhatsApp for everything. WhatsApp calls need the VPN.

      Backup: a Vodafone SIM at CAI arrivals, ≈$12, passport needed.

      Reference

      The boat, explained
      • A dahabiya is a sailing boat, not a cruise ship. 5–8 cabins, 10–16 guests, a crew of about ten, two lateen sails and a tug when the wind drops. Big cruise boats have 60 cabins, a buffet, a pool and a timetable. A dahabiya has a deck, a cook and time. It stops at islands, quarries and villages the big boats can't reach.
      • It leaves from Aswan, not Cairo, and runs downstream toward Luxor. The operator drives the last hour from Edfu or Esna.
      • Why Tuesday works on a dahabiya and not on a cruise boat: cruise boats keep fixed weekly timetables (Aswan departures Mon/Wed/Fri). Dahabiyas sail when their guests say.
      • The one thing to pin down when booking: the first night. The standard shared itinerary sails Tuesday after lunch. We want the boat to stay moored in Aswan Tuesday night so Abu Simbel at dawn Wednesday works and we sail at 1pm. A private charter does whatever we want; a shared boat may or may not. Ask it exactly that way.
      • Cost: four agencies quote $920–1,050 per person for 3 nights in a shared cabin, usually including temple tickets, guide, transfers and all meals. A regular cruise boat is $500–700 for the same nights with tickets extra, so the sail costs about $300 more than the ship.
      • Confirm each: cabin, all meals, Egyptologist guide, airport meet and transfers, Philae and High Dam on day 1, Kom Ombo, Gebel el-Silsila, Edfu, the transfer to Luxor. Ask whether entry fees are in (≈$35 if not). Abu Simbel is always extra: ≈$180 for the car and guide for two, plus ≈$25 each entry.
      • Before paying anything: get the boat's name and read its TripAdvisor page. "A 5-star dahabiya" is not a boat.
      • Where to book: email the Egyptian agencies directly (Egypt Tours Portal, Memphis Tours, Djed Egypt Travel) and ask two of them the same question. Deposit by card, balance in cash on board.
      • Wifi: none worth mentioning Tue–Fri. Tipping: ≈$60 each into the crew envelope on the last night, ≈$40 each for the guide.
      Book now vs. pay there

      Book now (fixed dates, peak season): the dahabiya, both domestic flights, GEM timed tickets, Khufu's, 1886, the Cairo guide and car. For the boat and the guide ask for deposit now, balance in cash on arrival. It's standard with reputable Egyptian agencies and it's our leverage.

      Book there, pay cash (cheaper, same product): the balloon (≈$80–90 at the hotel desk vs $120+ online), feluccas and motorboats, the Nubian village boat, a guide at Karnak, every tomb and temple ticket, the souk.

      • Never pay in full by bank wire to someone from Instagram. Card deposit with a company that has an address and a TripAdvisor history; balance in cash.
      • Viator, GetYourGuide and Trip.com add 15–25% over emailing the Egyptian agency directly. Same boat, same guide.
      • There is no "government tourist office" in Cairo. Anyone steering you to one is steering you to a shop.
      • Hotel desks mark tours up 50–100%. Fine for a $90 balloon; not for a $1,000 boat.
      • Agree the price of everything (ride, boat, photo, carriage) before it starts. Then it's a fair deal, not a scam.
      • Cards work at hotels, GEM, the big sites and on the boat. Cash for everything else.

      If something goes sideways
      • Sick. Pharmacies are good and cheap; El Ezaby and Seif are 24h in Cairo and Luxor and hand out antibiotics and rehydration salts over the counter. Hotel doctors come to the room (≈$50). Serious: As-Salam International Hospital in Cairo, Luxor International Hospital in Luxor. Ask the hotel, not Uber. Travel-insurance number saved in your phone before you fly.
      • Flight cancelled. EgyptAir rebooks at its own counter faster than by phone; domestic routes have several flights a day, so a cancellation costs hours, not days. International: call the airline from the hotel first.
      • Card eaten, ATM empty. This is why the USD is split between two pockets and two people. Hotels and the boat take cards; the other guy covers you until an ATM works.
      • Passport lost. Tourist police (126) for the report, then your embassy in Cairo for an emergency passport (same day for the US). A photo of the passport page on your phone turns a two-day problem into two hours.
      • Phone dead, no data. The Vodafone counter at arrivals and in every mall. The hotel desk will print the plan for you.
      • One of us is wiped out. The boat is the recovery day: everything on it is optional and every meal comes to you.
      • We fall out. Unlikely. But Luxor has two good bars, the Winter Palace terrace and the Hilton's riverside one. Go sit in the other one for an hour.

      Practical notes
      • Visa. e-Visa at visa2egypt.gov.eg ($25, allow a week). Backup: $25 USD cash at the arrivals bank counter before immigration, exact clean bills.
      • Baksheesh. Tipping is constant and expected. Small notes always in a pocket, not the wallet.
      • Touts. Relentless at Giza and the Khan, gentle in Aswan and Luxor. Friendly, firm, keep moving. No "free" camel photo, papyrus "gift" or "my cousin's shop is on the way".
      • Haggling. Open at a third of the ask. Smile. Walk away once.
      • Dress. Shoulders and knees covered for mosques and churches; temples don't care, the sun does. Light long sleeves beat SPF.
      • Water. Bottled only, teeth included. Ice at hotels and on the boat is fine.
      • Heat. Aswan and Abu Simbel hit 95°F by 11am in October. Everything big is before 10 for a reason. Electrolytes in the bag.
      • Photos. Most sites allow phones free; some charge for cameras. None inside Nefertari's or Seti's.
      • Alcohol. Hotels and the boat, yes; restaurants, sometimes; the street, no.

      Plan B if no boat will overnight in Aswan on Tuesday: board Wednesday instead, Old Cataract Tuesday night, Hilton Luxor Saturday only. Same trip, one fewer Luxor night, one more Aswan night.
